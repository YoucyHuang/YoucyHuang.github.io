Paintings

Fifteen Years of Practice

My painting developed alongside my thinking. From early realism through modern and postmodern turns — Descartes, Kant, Heidegger, postcolonial theory — each shift in understanding changed what appeared on the canvas. This is not a portfolio of finished works. It is the record of a mind and a body learning to see.

Stage 1  ·  Early Practice

Childhood & Adolescence

I have loved painting and fiction since I was a child. These early works — from age nine through seventeen — are not sophisticated, but they carry something that academic training later tried to refine but never replace: the instinct to make marks that mean something.

Stage 2  ·  2021–2022

Academic Training & the Classical Ideal

At the China Academy of Art, I began formal academic training. My early intention was to reproduce the visible world as faithfully as possible — building a three-dimensional space on a two-dimensional plane, following the lineage of Greek art through Da Vinci and Michelangelo. I was reading Plato, then Kant: the world can be mirrored, but we can only know phenomena, never the thing-in-itself. At the same time, I became obsessed with Cézanne — the first artist who showed me that painting is not about reproducing vision, but about constructing it.

Stage 3  ·  2023

The Heidegger Turn

In 2023, I took a German A1 course online — not for career planning, but because I was reading Heidegger and certain concepts like Dasein cannot be translated into Chinese without losing their weight. His essay “The Origin of Art” changed how I understood what painting is: not representation, not expression, but a happening of truth — an event in which a world opens. I began to understand my own practice not as making pictures, but as creating conditions for presence.

Stage 4  ·  2024

Authenticity Series

Heidegger’s distinction between authentic and inauthentic existence — between Dasein and das Man — led me to a series of portraits. I wanted to capture people not as they perform themselves for others, but as they are in moments when the performance drops. These paintings are not about likeness. They are about the quiet interval between one mask and the next.

Stage 5  ·  2024–2025

Chinese Landscape, Western Language

After studying postcolonial theory, I realized something uncomfortable: I knew more about Western culture than the culture I grew up in. Most of the time, I had been looking at my own tradition through a Western lens. I began to reread Chinese art history seriously, and to ask: how do you build a bridge that lets contemporary viewers — Chinese and non-Chinese alike — enter the world that ancient painters created? My answer became a series of experiments: classic Chinese landscape composition filtered through Western painters I love — Twombly, Robert Ryman, Cézanne. Traditional Chinese painting uses “moving focus,” not optical perspective. It is not a window. It is a walk. My semi-transparent mountain series is an attempt to make that walk visible.

Stage 6  ·  2025–2026

Graduation Work & the Turn to DH

My MFA thesis examined Robert Ryman — not as a minimalist, but as a painter who understood that the act of painting is fundamentally about body, trace, and presence. My graduation creation continued the semi-transparent mountain series. In May 2026, I participated in a rural revitalization project, deployed an AI agent on my computer, and built small interactive websites for the first time. That experience opened the door to Digital Humanities — not as a departure from art, but as a new field for old questions.
